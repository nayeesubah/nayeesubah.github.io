Nayee Subah Foundation is pleased to release its Annual Report for the financial year 2024–25. This report provides a comprehensive overview of our programs, financial performance, governance practices, and the measurable impact we have achieved together with our supporters, volunteers, and partner communities.

This year marked a significant inflection point for the foundation — we expanded our geographic reach, deepened our programmatic impact, and strengthened our organizational capacity for sustained growth.

## Key Impact Highlights

| Metric | FY 2024–25 |
|--------|-----------|
| Communities served | 15,000+ beneficiaries across 45 villages |
| Education | 5 new scholarship programs, 800+ children supported |
| Healthcare | 4 major health camps, 5,000+ patients treated |
| Women's empowerment | 150+ women trained in livelihood skills, 50 businesses launched |
| Community development | 3 community libraries established, 20 water filtration units installed |
| Skill development | 200+ youth enrolled in vocational training programs |
| Investment portfolio | ₹95 lakh invested across 4 sustainable projects |

## Financial Summary

The foundation maintained strong financial health and discipline throughout the year:

## Our Commitment to Transparency

We believe transparency is the foundation of trust. In keeping with that commitment:

- **Audited financial statements** are available for public review.
- Detailed month-wise income and expense breakdowns are published on our **[Transparency page](/transparency/)** and updated quarterly.
- All board meeting minutes and governance documents are maintained and accessible.

The report has been audited by [Auditor Name], and we have received an unqualified audit opinion — confirming that our financial statements present a true and fair view of the foundation's affairs.

## Governance Highlights

- The foundation's Governing Board held 6 meetings during the year.
- Two new board members with expertise in finance and rural development were inducted.
- A formal whistleblower policy and conflict-of-interest policy were adopted.
- An internal audit committee was constituted to strengthen financial oversight.

## Voices from the Field

> "Before Nayee Subah came to our village, we had no school, no health center, and no hope. Now my children go to school, and I have learned to read and write at the adult literacy classes." — Village elder, Dumka district

> "The skill development center changed my life. I completed the electrician course and now work with a construction company in Godda. I earn ₹12,000 a month and support my family." — Rajesh Kumar, graduate of the vocational training program

## Looking Forward: Goals for FY 2025–26

Building on this year's momentum, we have set ambitious targets for the coming year:

1. **Expand geographic reach** to 20 new villages in Pakur and Sahebganj districts.
2. **Launch a mobile health clinic** to serve the most remote communities.
3. **Establish 5 additional community libraries** across Jharkhand.
4. **Double the scholarship program** to support 1,600 children.
5. **Grow the investment portfolio** to ₹2 crore to generate sustainable funding.
6. **Scale the women's entrepreneurship program** to train 200 women.
7. **Achieve 100% digital financial reporting** for real-time transparency.

## A Note of Gratitude
