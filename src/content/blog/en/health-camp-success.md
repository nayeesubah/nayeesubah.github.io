On February 22–23, 2025, Nayee Subah Foundation organized a two-day free health camp in the Sahibganj district of Jharkhand, bringing essential healthcare services to remote villages where medical facilities are scarce or entirely absent. The camp was staffed by a team of 12 doctors, 8 nurses, and over 30 community volunteers.

The response was overwhelming: **over 1,200 patients** were examined, treated, and counseled over the two days — a clear indication of the urgent healthcare needs in the region.

## The Healthcare Gap in Rural Sahibganj

The nearest primary health center (PHC) is over **15 kilometers** from most of the villages served by this camp. For families dependent on daily wages, traveling that distance means losing a day's income — an impossible choice between health and livelihood. Many residents admitted they had not received any medical checkup in over a year.

"Our village has no doctor. If someone gets seriously ill at night, we have to arrange a private vehicle, which costs ₹1,000–1,500 just to reach the town hospital," says Meena Devi, a mother of three from a small hamlet 8 kilometers from the camp location. "This camp was a blessing for us."

## Comprehensive Services Provided

The camp offered a wide range of medical services, all completely free of charge:

| Service | Patients Served |
|---------|----------------|
| General health checkups | 1,200+ |
| Eye examinations | 450 (120 pairs of glasses distributed) |
| Dental checkups and treatment | 300 |
| Women's health consultations | 200 |
| Child immunization | 150 children vaccinated |
| Free medicines distributed | ₹2.35 lakh worth |

### Specialized Care and Follow-Ups

The camp identified **35 individuals** requiring specialized follow-up care for conditions such as cataracts, suspected tuberculosis, hypertension complications, and severe anemia. These patients have been referred to partner hospitals in Dumka and Godda for further evaluation and treatment at no cost.

The foundation's medical coordinator notes: "Many of these conditions are treatable if caught early. The problem is that people in remote areas simply don't have access to regular screening. Camps like this are not just about treatment — they are about early detection and prevention."

## Health Awareness: Prevention Is Better Than Cure

Beyond diagnosis and treatment, the camp placed strong emphasis on health education. Interactive awareness sessions were conducted on:

- **Hygiene and sanitation:** Handwashing techniques, safe drinking water practices, and toilet usage.
- **Maternal and child nutrition:** Dietary guidelines for pregnant women and young children, addressing the high prevalence of anemia in the region.
- **Prevention of waterborne diseases:** Recognizing symptoms of typhoid, cholera, and jaundice, and understanding when to seek medical help.
- **Family planning and reproductive health:** Counseling sessions for women of reproductive age, with distribution of contraceptives.

Health workers distributed **500+ informational pamphlets** in Hindi and Santhali (the local tribal language) to ensure the messages reached every household.

## Voices from the Camp

> "I have been suffering from blurry vision for two years. I thought it was just age. Today, the doctor told me I have cataracts and arranged for free surgery. I can't express what this means to me." — Budhni Devi, 62, cataract patient referred for surgery

> "My three-year-old son had not received his full vaccination schedule because the PHC is too far. Today he got his remaining shots. I feel relieved." — Sita Murmu, young mother from a neighboring village

## Looking Ahead: Sustainable Healthcare Access

Given the overwhelming response, the foundation has committed to expanding its healthcare initiatives:

- **Quarterly health camps** in the Sahibganj and Dumka regions, rotating through different village clusters.
- **Mobile health clinic:** The foundation is actively raising funds for a fully equipped mobile medical van that can reach the most remote hamlets on a regular schedule.
- **Telemedicine pilot:** Exploring partnerships to enable video consultations between villagers and specialist doctors in Ranchi and Patna.
- **Community health worker training:** Training local volunteers to serve as first responders and health educators in their own villages.
