In rural Jharkhand, where women face some of the highest barriers to economic participation in India, a quiet transformation is underway. Fifty women from 12 self-help groups (SHGs) across the Dumka, Godda, and Pakur districts have successfully completed Nayee Subah Foundation's three-month Entrepreneurship Development Program — and launched their own micro-enterprises.

For these women, the journey from self-help to self-reliance represents not just economic independence, but a fundamental shift in their roles within their families and communities.

## Bridging the Opportunity Gap

Women in rural Jharkhand face a stark reality: low literacy rates, limited mobility, restricted access to financial services, and deep-rooted social norms that confine them to unpaid domestic work. According to the National Family Health Survey-5 (2019–21), only **63% of women in Jharkhand are literate**, and female labor force participation in the state stands at a mere **23%**.

The Entrepreneurship Development Program was designed to address these barriers holistically — combining skill training, financial literacy, mentorship, and market linkages in a single structured intervention.

## What the Program Covered

Over three months, participants engaged in intensive training modules:

### Financial Literacy
- Basic accounting and bookkeeping for small businesses
- Understanding savings, credit, and interest
- Accessing micro-finance and government schemes (Mudra loans, Stand-Up India, etc.)
- Digital payments and mobile banking

### Vocational Skills
Participants chose from four specialized tracks based on local market demand and personal interest:
- **Tailoring and garment making** — 18 women
- **Food processing** (pickles, papad, spice powders) — 14 women
- **Handicrafts and traditional jewelry** — 10 women
- **Mushroom and poultry farming** — 8 women

### Business Management
- Pricing strategies and cost calculation
- Branding, packaging, and product presentation
- Customer relations and market research
- Basics of social media marketing using WhatsApp and Facebook

## Real Stories, Real Impact

### Sunita Devi: From Struggle to Self-Sufficiency

Sunita Devi, a 32-year-old mother of two from a village in Godda district, joined the program with little more than determination. Her husband's income as a daily wage laborer was irregular, and the family often struggled to afford two meals a day.

"I used to stay awake at night worrying about how to feed my children," Sunita recalls. "I had learned basic tailoring as a girl but had no machine, no capital, and no confidence to start a business."

Through the program, Sunita received a sewing machine, training in advanced stitching and design, and a small startup loan of ₹15,000 from the group's revolving fund. Today, she runs a tailoring shop from her home, earning **₹6,000–8,000 per month**. She has even hired two other women from her village to help fulfill orders.

"I never imagined I would be an employer," she says with a smile. "Now my children go to school regularly, and we have savings for the first time."

### The Mushroom Farmers of Pakur

A group of 10 women in Pakur district took an unconventional path: collectively establishing a mushroom farming enterprise. With training from the foundation's agriculture partner and a small shared investment, they now produce oyster mushrooms on a rotational basis.

The enterprise supplies fresh mushrooms to local markets and has secured a supply contract with a regional supermarket chain. The women rotate duties — growing, harvesting, packaging, and sales — and share profits equally.

"We were laughed at initially," says the group's leader, Phoolo Devi. "'Women can't farm mushrooms,' people said. Now the same people come to us to learn."

## Measurable Outcomes

- **50 women** trained and certified
- **48 businesses** currently operational (96% success rate)
- **Average monthly income increase of ₹4,500** per woman
- **12 new self-help groups** formed as a result of the program
- **3 group enterprises** registered as formal businesses
- **₹50,000+** in collective monthly earnings across all participants

## Sustainability and Scaling

The program was designed for long-term impact, not one-time intervention:

- **Ongoing mentorship:** Foundation staff conduct monthly follow-up visits to each entrepreneur, providing guidance on challenges they face.
- **Revolving fund:** A ₹2 lakh revolving fund provides small loans (₹5,000–₹25,000) to graduates for working capital or equipment purchases. As loans are repaid, the fund supports new batches of trainees.
- **Market linkages:** The foundation is building relationships with local retailers, hotels, and government procurement programs to create stable market access for the women's products.
- **Peer learning network:** Monthly meetings bring together all 50 entrepreneurs to share experiences, solve problems collectively, and build solidarity.

> "When you empower a woman, you empower an entire community. She educates her children, improves her family's health, and becomes a role model for other women. This is how change happens — one woman at a time." — Program Coordinator, Nayee Subah Foundation

## The Road Ahead

The foundation plans to run two more cohorts of the Entrepreneurship Development Program in 2025, targeting an additional 100 women. We are also exploring partnerships with e-commerce platforms to help the women sell their products online.
