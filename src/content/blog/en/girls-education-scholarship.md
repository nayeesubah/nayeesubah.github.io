Every morning, 13-year-old Puja Kumari walks four kilometers from her village in Giridih to reach the nearest upper-primary school. Until last year, that walk would have been the end of the road for her education. But this year, with the help of the Udaan scholarship program run by Nayee Subah Foundation, Puja is not only still in school — she ranks among the top five students in her class.

Puja is one of **75 girls** currently supported by Udaan, our initiative to keep girls in school at a stage when dropout rates spike the most: the transition from primary to upper-primary and secondary school.

## Why Girls Drop Out in Rural Jharkhand

The reasons girls leave school in rural Jharkhand are rarely about ability. They are about cost, distance, and expectation:

- **Cost of schooling:** Even in government schools, the cost of books, uniforms, stationery, and exam fees adds up to several thousand rupees a year — a serious burden for daily-wage families.
- **Distance and safety:** For many girls, the nearest secondary school is kilometers away, and families worry about the safety of the commute.
- **Early marriage pressure:** Jharkhand has one of the highest rates of child marriage in India. Families facing financial strain often see marriage as the only option for a daughter's future.
- **Household responsibility:** Girls are often expected to help with chores, younger siblings, and farm work instead of attending school.

Our field research showed that a small, predictable scholarship — combined with a mentor who checks in regularly — dramatically changes the odds that a girl stays in school.

## How Udaan Works

The program takes a simple, wrap-around approach rather than just writing a cheque:

### Financial Support

Each scholar receives support covering school fees, textbooks, uniforms, and essential supplies. This is paid directly to the school or the supplier, so the money is always spent on education.

### One-on-One Mentoring

Every girl is paired with a local woman mentor — often a teacher, a self-help group leader, or a graduate from our community. Mentors visit monthly, track attendance, and provide guidance on studies and life decisions. This personal connection is what families often cite as the program's most valuable part.

### Parent Engagement

We hold parent meetings each term to explain the long-term returns of education and to address concerns about safety and livelihood. These conversations have shifted attitudes measurably — 60% of parents in the current cohort say they now see a daughter's education as a priority investment.

### After-School Support

Two after-school learning centers in the region provide a safe space for homework help, exam preparation, and computer basics — keeping scholars engaged even outside classroom hours.

## The Impact So Far

The results of the first full academic year are encouraging:

| Outcome | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Girls retained in school | 75 of 75 (100%) |
| Attendance rate | 92% average |
| Passing exam rate | 88% |
| Girls promoted to next grade | 71 |
| Families engaged in parent meetings | 68 |

Perhaps most tellingly, **14 families that were considering early marriage for a daughter have chosen to postpone it** and keep her enrolled after learning about the scholarship and mentorship available.

## What It Takes to Scale

Udaan's model is simple, but it is not free. The full cost of supporting one girl for a year — fees, materials, mentoring stipend, and learning-center costs — comes to approximately **₹6,500**. At that cost, scaling to 150, 300, or 500 girls is entirely a matter of funding, not of approach.
